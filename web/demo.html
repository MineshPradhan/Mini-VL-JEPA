What people wrote

    Browse the dataset

    Held-out MSR-VTT clips (Val + Test) the model never saw in training.

    Walk the pipeline

    Step through one stage at a time, or press Show all to see the whole diagram at once. Arrow keys work too.

    Frozen ❄ blocks are downloaded, never updated. Only the two 🔥 blocks carry gradients, which is why the whole thing trains on one 6 GB laptop GPU.

    Eight languages for free

    Train the predictor on English captions only; query it in Hindi, German, Tamil, Chinese, Telugu, Marathi, Arabic and Bengali, with 83–97% of English retrieval retained. No translated training data, no second training run.vs. DistilGPT-2 baseline: English only

    The decoder mostly sleeps

    Consecutive windows of a video usually mean the same thing. Comparing predicted embeddings costs a dot product; generating text costs an autoregressive pass. Skipping the redundant ones gives 3.1× on streaming input at τ = 0.10.vs. VL-JEPA / video-LLMs: decode every window

    Trains on hardware a student owns

    Both large models stay frozen and their outputs are cached, so a single training epoch touches only a 26.5 M-parameter head. 6 GB VRAM, no cluster, no gradient checkpointing gymnastics.vs. CLIP4Clip, InternVideo2: 8–64 A100s

    The predictor picks the caption

    Generation is the weak link, retrieval is the strong one. So the decoder proposes several captions and the predictor's own embedding chooses between them — the strong head grades the weak one.vs. beam log-probability alone

    Swappable target space

    Nothing in the head is tied to LaBSE. Point it at another sentence encoder and the whole language coverage changes without touching the video side.vs. jointly-trained dual encoders

    Reproducible end to end

    Cached features are deterministic, the head is small, and a full re-run is hours rather than GPU-weeks — so every ablation in the thesis can actually be re-run by an examiner.

    Retrieval sits well below the dual encoders

    A contrastively trained pair of encoders that sees text during video training will beat a regression head onto a frozen text space. The gap is the price of freezing both towers.

    Captions are fluent, not precise

    The head predicts one sentence vector; fine detail — counts, colours, names — is not recoverable from it. Expect plausible captions with occasional wrong specifics.

    Hindi output lags Hindi retrieval

    Cross-lingual retrieval is strong because LaBSE is strong. Hindi generation is limited by mT5-small under LoRA, not by the predictor — a decoder problem, and a fixable one.

    The gate assumes slow scenes

    τ trades captions-per-second against missed transitions. On rapid cuts the same τ either fires constantly (no saving) or skips a real change (stale caption).

    One dataset, one domain

    MSR-VTT is short, web-sourced, English-captioned clips. Nothing here has been shown to hold on egocentric, medical or surveillance video.

    Inherits every bias upstream

    Both frozen models carry their pre-training distribution. Freezing them means their blind spots cannot be trained away — they can only be measured.

    Limitations are what the method cannot do. These are the places where a named alternative does the same job better, and why we accepted the trade.

    No dialogue, no questions, no reasoning

    Ask Qwen2-VL or VideoChat2 "what colour is the pan?" and it answers. This emits one caption and stops. It is a captioner, not an assistant, and no amount of head training changes that.loses to: Qwen2-VL, Video-LLaMA, VideoChat2

    Retrieval trails a tuned dual encoder

    CLIP4Clip and X-CLIP see text while training the video tower, so their two spaces are shaped for each other. We regress onto a text space that was frozen before we started. Freezing buys the 6 GB budget and costs retrieval accuracy.loses to: CLIP4Clip, X-CLIP, InternVideo2

    Generation quality is capped by mT5-small

    The predicted embedding carries more than the decoder can express. A 7B LLM head on the same vector would read better. mT5-small was chosen for VRAM, not for fluency — the ceiling is ours, not the method's.loses to: any LLM-headed video model

    One vector per window loses ordering

    Pooling to a single embedding discards sequence. "He picks it up, then puts it down" and its reverse land in almost the same place. Token-level video-LLMs keep the frames and can tell them apart.loses to: frame-token architectures

    The gate's saving is scene-dependent

    The 3.1× holds on MSR-VTT's slow web clips. On rapid cuts the gate fires on nearly every window and the speed-up collapses toward 1×. It is a real result on a real distribution, not a universal constant.worst case: equal to ungated decoding

    Two-stage training needs a workaround

    The decoder trains on ground-truth LaBSE vectors but meets predicted ones at inference, and early captions were poor because of it. Predicted-embedding mixing plus noise injection closes the gap, but the mismatch is inherent to decoupling the two heads.end-to-end models do not have this seam

    Multilingual video search

    Index once with the predicted embedding, query in any LaBSE language. No per-language index, no translation service in the request path.

    Live captioning on an edge device

    The gate is what makes this plausible: encode continuously, generate rarely. Cost tracks scene changes rather than wall-clock time.

    Archive triage

    Embed a large unlabelled collection, cluster in the shared space, and read out only the cluster centroids as captions.

    Cheap probe for a new video encoder

    Swap the frozen backbone, retrain the head in an afternoon, and read off how much language-alignable content the new features carry.

    Teaching artifact

    Every stage is separable and inspectable — a rare thing in a video-language stack, and the reason the gate can be drawn live in this page.

    Not for

    Anything needing exact reading of on-screen text, counting, safety-critical description, or fine-grained action boundaries.
